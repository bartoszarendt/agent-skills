# Assets and media

Use this reference when choosing, creating, capturing, or replacing imagery and
media, or when cropping and delivery affect the result. Work within the requested
deliverable, available rights, access, and tools. An asset task does not by itself
authorize installing software, accessing private accounts, or spending on a service.

## Choose what the user needs to see

Identify each asset's purpose: demonstrate the product, explain a relationship,
show the actual subject, support identification, or establish an intended mood.
Choose a source and form that serve that purpose:

| Need | Consider | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The actual product or work | Suitable supplied material or an authorized capture | Accuracy, relevant detail, and privacy |
| A concept that does not yet exist | A clearly identified mockup or generated asset | An honest distinction between proposal and reality |
| A place, person, object, or activity | Relevant supplied or appropriately licensed imagery | Correct subject and attribution |
| A process, structure, or comparison | A diagram, illustration, or chart where it explains the content | Labels, meaning, and accessible alternatives |
| Words or data that already carry the message | Typography or the actual information | Readability without a decorative image |

Inspect suitable supplied assets before seeking replacements. Prefer an actual
product capture when the task calls for demonstrating an existing interface.
Use a concept when it is the requested deliverable or the product does not yet
exist, and identify it visibly enough to avoid a misleading claim. A real capture
is not mandatory when an explanatory diagram better serves the task.

Test whether an image contributes understanding or the intended identity. If
removing it changes neither, consider omitting it. A purposeful atmospheric image
can be appropriate for a brand or editorial brief; do not force an invented fact
into its caption to justify it. Avoid off-subject imagery used only to fill space.

## Check rights and representations

Verify permission and applicable license conditions for the intended use. Check
attribution, modification, distribution, and any relevant restrictions at the
asset's source. Do not assume that public availability, a search result, a free
download, or a generated file establishes unrestricted rights.

Retain required credits and notices in the delivered form. Keep enough source
information in the project's existing asset records to reconstruct permission
where appropriate. Do not invent image URLs or infer a license from a provider's
general reputation. Use suitable available material or state the missing asset
when rights cannot be established.

Keep representations truthful. Do not imply that a stock person is an employee,
customer, or endorser, or that a generic location is the client's premises. Label
stand-ins and sample content appropriately. Do not alter a product capture to
suggest behavior, results, or data the product does not provide.

## Capture a product safely and accurately

Use an available, authorized browser or application capture mechanism when a
capture will improve the deliverable. Inspect the existing project and available
runtime before starting anything. Starting a local development process may fit
the implementation request; installing dependencies, entering accounts, changing
shared data, or using a paid service needs authorization covering those effects.

Choose a state that demonstrates the relevant task. Prefer suitable sample data
or an existing demonstration environment over personal or customer records. Do
not create or change records merely to improve a picture unless that action is
authorized. If access or a required state is unavailable, use supplied material,
an honestly identified concept, or report the specific gap.

Inspect the capture for names, email addresses, credentials, account details,
private paths, debug overlays, and unintended notifications. Exclude sensitive
content from the captured state or capture a safe region. If authorized redaction
is needed, make it preserve the truth of the demonstration. A screenshot can
contain sensitive material even when it is visually small in the final design.

Capture at a size that retains relevant detail at the displayed dimensions.
Prefer a crop of the actual feature over shrinking an entire screen until its
text becomes unreadable. Inspect the resulting file; a successful capture command
does not prove that the intended screen or state was captured. Stop temporary
processes you started when they are no longer needed.

## Design the crop and frame

Choose aspect ratio and placement from the subject and surrounding content.
Identify the focal point and inspect it in each materially different crop. A
portrait may survive a narrow card but lose its face in a wide banner; a wide
interface capture may need a different detail on a phone.

Keep meaningful content intact. Use a distinct crop or arrangement when necessary
instead of assuming one centered cover crop fits every frame. Let decorative
imagery crop more freely than a diagram, product detail, or image containing
information the user must read.

Inspect text over the actual image region in relevant crops and states. Use
placement, a suitable backing surface, or a measured treatment to keep it legible.
Do not rely on a quiet area that disappears in the narrow crop. Keep essential
text available as text, and provide an appropriate alternative for meaningful
visual information. Decorative images should not add redundant announcements.

Use frames, icons, and treatments consistent with the product's asset system.
Device frames, illustrations, and generated images are options when they explain
the subject or serve the brief; their presence or absence is not a quality gate.

## Deliver assets for their actual rendering

Reserve layout space when dimensions or aspect ratio are known. Select image
resolution and compression for displayed size, pixel density, acceptable detail,
and the product's performance needs. Avoid sending a full-resolution original
for every thumbnail or enlarging a low-resolution capture beyond useful clarity.

On the web, use responsive sources where useful and make their sizing information
match the actual layout. Use art direction when different crops are needed,
rather than treating a higher-resolution file as the answer to every viewport.
Prioritize assets needed for the initial view and defer others when appropriate;
do not lazy-load the principal image without considering its rendering cost.
Verify the delivered file, source selection, and layout rather than relying on
attributes alone.

For video or animated media, provide suitable controls, alternatives, and captions
where the content requires them. Respect motion preferences, preserve essential
information without autoplay, and account for load failures. Use available tools
and the existing delivery stack; adding a media service or library needs a concrete
benefit. If a tool is unavailable, preserve a usable asset treatment and report
what remains unproduced or unverified.

## Worked example: a product capture in a narrow layout

Example brief: a planning product supplies a wide calendar capture and requests a
page that demonstrates conflict resolution on desktop and phone.

Inspect whether the supplied capture actually shows that task. If an authorized
demo is available, capture the relevant calendar and conflict panel with sample
content. Otherwise use the supplied capture where it is accurate and identify
any proposed state as a concept; do not paint a working resolution onto it.

Show enough calendar context on desktop to explain the conflict. On a phone,
use a readable detail or a stacked explanation instead of scaling the whole
desktop screen into a tiny frame. Keep the event labels needed to understand
the example, reserve the frame's space, and describe the demonstrated behavior
in adjacent text. Inspect both crops and confirm that no private event details
remain. Record any temporary capture or replacement asset that the handoff needs.

## Verification

- [ ] Each chosen asset serves the content or intended identity and represents its subject honestly.
- [ ] Rights, attribution, and any relevant restrictions fit the delivered use.
- [ ] Captures use authorized access and reveal no unintended sensitive material.
- [ ] Important subjects and information remain legible in representative crops and sizes.
- [ ] Dimensions, responsive delivery, loading, and meaningful alternatives were checked where applicable.
- [ ] Temporary assets, unavailable tools, and remaining production gaps are identified.
