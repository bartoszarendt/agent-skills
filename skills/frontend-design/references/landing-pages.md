# Landing pages and portfolios

Use these considerations for marketing and editorial surfaces. Choose structure
from the audience, message, and available content, while preserving the brief.

## Main message and action

Make the product or subject identifiable and the primary action easy to find.
Fit the most useful content into the initial view where practical, without
forcing copy or typography into a fixed line count.

Use imagery when it explains the subject, demonstrates the product, or establishes
the intended identity. A text-led page can be complete when the message carries
the design. Avoid adding a decorative visual solely to satisfy a template.

Keep navigation understandable and usable at supported widths. Shorten labels
only when their meaning survives. Do not silently remove useful routes to fit
a preferred layout.

## Structure and density

Group information by the reader's questions and decisions. Vary layouts when the
content needs a different relationship; repeat them when consistency aids scanning.

Use lists for comparable items, tables for comparisons, cards for independent
units, and sequence markers for actual sequences. Do not replace a clear list
with a carousel merely because it contains more than a fixed number of items.

Keep spacing, headings, and emphasis consistent with hierarchy. Choose density
based on the amount of useful information and supported viewport. Define a
responsive treatment for every multi-column composition.

## Content and proof

Use precise claims supported by supplied or verified information. Label sample
metrics, testimonials, logos, and product mockups. Do not imply customer
relationships or endorsements without evidence.

Prefer descriptive CTA labels and consistent names for the same action. Keep
supporting copy concise without dropping information needed to decide.

Place proof near the claim it supports. A logo row, quote, demonstration, or
comparison should serve a purpose; none is mandatory.

## Assets and motion

Use imagery with the right subject, crop, resolution, and rights for the task.
Preserve required attribution. Ensure text and marks remain legible in each
supported scheme.

Use motion only when it contributes to understanding or feedback. Avoid
interactions that obscure content, interfere with navigation, or disregard
reduced-motion preferences.

## Verification

- [ ] The subject, value, and primary action are clear.
- [ ] Structure follows the content without arbitrary quotas or hidden information.
- [ ] Claims and proof are accurate; samples and temporary assets are identified.
- [ ] Navigation and actions work at supported widths and input modes.
- [ ] Layout, contrast, and motion meet the applicable accessibility requirements.
